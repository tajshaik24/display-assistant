Features

Feels like Apple built it.

Display Assistant talks to your monitor over DDC/CI, the same channel its on-screen menu uses, and wraps it in controls that look and behave like the rest of macOS.

Menu bar panel

Display and Sound sliders for every monitor, with the segmented track of the macOS volume indicator, a mute button and an HDR toggle.

Sync Brightness

All displays move together, so your monitor follows your MacBook's auto-brightness and brightness keys.

Your keyboard's keys

Brightness, mute and volume keys act on the display under the pointer, with the same on-screen indicator macOS shows.

Control Center

Add controls that open the sliders, jump to a brightness or volume you choose, or mute the display's speakers.

Shortcuts, Siri & Spotlight

Set or read brightness and volume, mute, and switch HDR — for example, from an automation when a movie app opens.

A command-line tool

displayctl handles brightness, volume, mute and HDR from your scripts. Build it from the source on GitHub.

Keyboard

The keys you already reach for.

Brightness keys follow the pointer to the right screen. Volume keys take over only while a display is your sound output — headphones and speakers work as usual.

Works with your setup

Every display, the right way.

Monitors macOS can't adjust get full control over DDC/CI. The built-in display, Studio Display, Pro Display XDR and LG UltraFine show up with a brightness slider while their keys and volume stay native. With HDR on, brightness goes through macOS so the slider and keys keep working.

For scripts

$ displayctl get brightness
62/100
$ displayctl set brightness 30
$ displayctl set volume 45
$ displayctl set mute 1
$ displayctl hdr on

Get started

Up and running in a minute.

Signed and notarized by Apple. Updates arrive on their own.

  1. Download and open the disk image

    Drag Display Assistant into your Applications folder, then open it.

  2. Enable keyboard control

    Click the display icon in the menu bar and press Enable to allow Accessibility access for the brightness and volume keys.

  3. Optional: add Control Center controls

    Open Control Center, choose Edit Controls and search for “Display”.
